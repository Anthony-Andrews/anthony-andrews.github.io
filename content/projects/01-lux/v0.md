version zero was the proof-of-concept board that started this whole project.

I based the initial PCB design heavily on the Radxa CM5-IO carrier schematic designed for a 2-lane OV9281 sensor.

I custom-designed the buck converter using TI's Webench calculator, but it was under-specced and unreliable under the noisy robot power.

I switched later revisions to an external Pololu module and ditched the OV9281 in favor of a higher-resolution AR0234 sensor (monochrome).

I ported PhotonVision to run on the camera to establish a point of comparison and rule out software as a potential factor.
[[Source code]](https://github.com/Lux-Robotics/allwpilib)


## Results:

- E2E latency dropped from ~110ms to ~35ms
- Stable 60fps at 1280@800 decimate 2 running (limited by mipi bandwidth)
- Under motion, the raw frames and improved performance meant vision data was accurate (when compared to wheel odometry)

## Shortcomings:
- Monochrome only limited options for object detection
- Overall compute utilization scaled poorly not taking full advantage of the faster SoC.
- Should have better quantified the detection range and accuracy improvements over the old sensors (OV2311) (more controlled tests)
- Need higher-bandwidth 4-lane mipi to push higher framerates
- Unreliable buck converter and physically large device size made integration harder
- Raw compute performance isn't everything, low-light performance was inconsistent and led to lighting-dependent pose, lens calibration was *crucial*. PnP ambiguity was common when only a single tag was in frame. 

After this I learned to start with the bottleneck for the robot and work backwards to what should be improved on the cameras.
